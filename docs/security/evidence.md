# Security review evidence

Phase 4 epic "Security review" (roadmap: threat-model walkthrough, pen-test checklist, evidence pack; exit gate: findings closed). Reviewed commit: `a70f63f` (main before the review). Method: [threat-model.md](threat-model.md), then the attacks in [pentest-checklist.md](pentest-checklist.md) written as automated tests; a finding is an attack that worked, or a control the design promises that the running code did not provide.

F-01 to F-18 are the Phase 4 review; F-19 to F-36 are a second, independent review of PRs #93 to #99 (below). ## Retention precedence (F-16, migration 0205)

Effective hours are `least(workspace, matter policy if there is one, otherwise vault policy or the vault's old setting)`, skipping unlimited values. A narrower scope can shorten the workspace limit, never lengthen it. A thread or review table has no vault; a file has one vault and at most one matter. Legal holds, the 3 hour minimum and the 30 day bin are unchanged. Fixtures that advance the clock: `api/retention/precedence.test.ts`.

What upgrading does to a running deployment:

- No expiry is stored. The estimate, the sweep and the warnings compute it when they run, so the first sweep after the upgrade (at server start) bins everything older than the workspace limit that a longer or unlimited vault or matter policy had been keeping. It is restorable for 30 days from then. Restoring restarts the clock for one workspace limit; to keep something longer, place a hold or lengthen the workspace policy. No vault warning goes out for what is already past due. Place holds before upgrading if anything must not move.
- A matter policy now replaces the vault's even when it is longer or unlimited (still within the workspace limit). Items binned earlier under the shorter vault hours stay in the bin and are purged on their date unless an admin restores them.
- Deletion jobs already queued keep the estimate computed under the old rule. They are records: the sweep selects objects afresh and marks them done.
- The newly eligible items go through the policy sweep, which always bins. At that release the vault job still deleted for good (F-37, fixed since in 0210).

## Vault retention setting (F-37, migration 0210)

`vaults.retention_hours` is applied only by the policy sweep, as that vault's policy until a policy row replaces it. Files it takes are handled like any other retention deletion: out of lists and search while binned, restorable for 30 days (recycle bin or vault bin), skipped under a legal hold, and removed with their index rows and stored bytes at the final purge. The vault job now only empties the vault bin. Nothing deleted for good before the upgrade comes back.

## Residual risk

1. A person with a valid session and the right to run SQL as the app role bypasses the synced-vault guard and the API-scope setting (F-07). The API has no such path; a new raw SQL route would be the way in.
2. A matter's `retention_hours` field is not enforced (F-38).
3. The tool executor and policy engine are covered end to end against a fake MCP server (`api/e2e/runs.test.ts`, `api/agents/tools.test.ts`), and were run by hand against the MCP project's reference server (`@modelcontextprotocol/server-everything` 1.0.0, streamable HTTP, on loopback), not against a vendor's server. Session ids and SSE replies are supported (`api/admin-models/mcp-transport.test.ts`); OAuth connectors, tool-list paging and requests a server makes of the client are not.
4. Single process in-memory limiters (F-15).
5. Everything marked not built above, in particular ADM-007 allow-listing and the injection suite, which matter most for a compromised-account or malicious-document actor.
